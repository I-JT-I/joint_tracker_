What is a tolerance break and why take one

With regular cannabis use, the body tends to get used to THC: you need larger amounts to get the same effect you felt the first few times. This is called tolerance, and it's the reason many regular users choose to take a tolerance break (often shortened to "t-break"): a period of voluntary abstinence meant to bring sensitivity back to where it started.

What happens during a tolerance break

In the first few days it's normal to notice a drop in appetite, trouble sleeping or irritability: these are temporary effects of interrupting a well-established habit, not a health problem. As the days go by, the body gradually clears the cannabinoids it has built up and sensitivity to their effects starts to return.

How long it should last

There's no magic number that works for everyone: it depends on how often and how much you used before. As a general reference:

  • A few days: a small reset for someone who has only recently started using daily.
  • 2-4 weeks: the most commonly cited window for a noticeable effect on tolerance in regular users.
  • More than a month: for someone with very frequent, long-term use.

The most reliable way to figure out what works for you is to look at your own real data, not a generic rule.

After the break

When you go back, it's a good idea to start with smaller amounts than usual: your tolerance has dropped, so the same amount as before will have a stronger effect. Logging your post-break sessions helps you find your balance again quickly.
