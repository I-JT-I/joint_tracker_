How to cut down on cannabis use: a practical guide

Cutting down on cannabis is easier when you start from a measurable goal rather than a vague "smoke less". Here's a practical approach, step by step.

1. Start from real data, not perception

Most people either underestimate or overestimate how much they actually use. Before changing anything, spend a week or two logging your sessions exactly as they are today: how many, when, how many grams. It works as an objective starting point.

2. Spot the patterns, not just the numbers

Looking at your history, clear patterns often show up: sessions clustered in the evening, on weekends, or tied to a specific context (boredom, socializing, trouble sleeping). Knowing when and why you use more is more useful than the monthly total on its own.

3. Set a gradual, realistic goal

Cutting 10-20% from your recorded real average is a more sustainable goal than a drastic cut overnight. Small, steady reductions are easier to keep up over time than a sharp change.

4. Replace the recurring triggers

If the data shows you mostly use to unwind in the evening or to fall asleep, finding a specific alternative for that moment (a different routine, exercise, something else) works better than relying on willpower alone in the abstract.

5. Consider a tolerance break as a reset

A planned break (see what is a tolerance break) can be a useful starting point for picking up again with smaller amounts, since reduced tolerance means smaller doses are enough.

6. Measure your progress over time

Comparing grams used, number of sessions and spending month over month helps you see whether the reduction is actually working, instead of relying on a subjective feeling. An app like JointTracker works out these stats automatically as you log your sessions.
